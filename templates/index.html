How sleep shapes memory

Sleep is far from a passive state. Through the night the brain replays what it took in during the day, moving new memories into long-term storage where they are less likely to fade.

In study after study, people who slept after learning recalled more than those who stayed awake. Deep sleep seems to lock in facts, while dream-heavy REM sleep helps link ideas together.

Short nights take a toll. Without enough rest, the brain struggles to form new memories, and even a single bad night can make focus slip the next day.
